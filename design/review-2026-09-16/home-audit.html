Home — why it doesn't feel like onboarding
Six concrete deviations from the design language the onboarding screens are built on.

1

No hero — the biggest text is 15px

The greeting is the page title, set at AppType.bodyStrong (15px). Onboarding anchors every screen with a 22–26px w800 headline. Nothing here says “this is the screen”.

2

Eight co-equal modules, no hierarchy

Greeting + 3 status pills, week strip, Today ring, Protein, Steps, Water, quests, log card — all the same visual weight in one scroll. Onboarding gives one idea the whole screen.

_header() · week strip · _homePage() children
3

Small type everywhere

~40 sites at body/small (13–15px); no large anchor anywhere below the ring. Density without a focal point is what reads as “cramped dashboard”.

AppType.body / AppType.small across Home
4

Too many hues

The palette is cream + near-black + orange. Home adds pink (Sugar), blue (Sodium), amber (Burn) and green (steps / Track). Onboarding uses the orange accent alone.

Sugar / Sodium / Burn chips · Track pill
5

Icons, never illustration

Every module is a small Material Symbol. Onboarding is illustration-led at hero scale (the Leonardo art). Home has no illustration at all.

protein / steps / water module icons
6

The nav covers the primary action

The floating nav is a Stack overlay (Positioned, bottom: 14, 64px). At first paint it sits on the “What's cooking?” card — clipping the title and the Scan food tiles. The main thing you came to do is half-hidden.

home_screen.dart:547 · FloatingNav overlay
THE ONE LINE

Onboarding is one idea, big left-aligned type, generous space, illustration. Home is the opposite: many small cards, small type, tight gaps, icons. Same app, two different languages.
