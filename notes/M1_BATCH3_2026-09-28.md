# M1 batch 3 — the score got worse, and that is the finding

**Landed 2026-09-28.** 5 more photos in `tools/ai_accuracy/photos/`,
`apply_batch3.py`, `baseline_batch3_2026-09-28.json`. Corpus **15 photos / 17
cases**.

## What I did NOT change

The app. The prompt. The Worker. Nothing. This batch only added photos.

**So the movement in the score is a change in what is measured, not a change in
the product.** Producer `tools/ai_accuracy.py`, model
`gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18` via `AICREDITS_KEY`, 17 scans, **17 answered, 0
rate-limited, 0 retries**, run 2026-09-28.

| | batch 2 (12 cases) | batch 3 (17 cases) |
|---|---|---|
| median calorie error | 15.4% | **20.0%** |

**This is not a regression in the app and not an improvement.** Nothing shipped
between the two runs. It means the earlier 15.4% was flattered by an easier
corpus, and I should be suspicious of it for exactly that reason.

## The trap, for the third time

Of 18 candidates fetched, **8 were masala dosa and 7 were idli.** Commons returns
whatever a dish is famous for. Taken as harvested, the corpus would have been
five dosas in a row and would have scored beautifully while proving nothing.
Taken as curated: 5 distinct dishes, 13 rejected with reasons (2 hotel/food-court
shots, one with a person's hand in frame, the rest duplicates).

## The honest limit of this batch

The queries were breakfast items, so **all five accepted photos are
South-Indian breakfast.** That is a real bias, recorded in `cases.json` rather
than hidden. Batch 4 must cover curries, eggs and non-breakfast home cooking
before any overall score is quoted to anyone.

## What is actually wrong — the useful part

The five new cases are the worst in the corpus, and they fail the same way:

| case | error | direction | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tamil_uthappam` | **61.3%** | over | **missed "Uthappam"** and invented a dish instead |
| `idli_sambar_chutney_plate` | **56.0%** | over | — |
| `masala_dosa_plate` | **55.2%** | over | invented 1 |
| `rava_idli_home` | 32.1% | over | missed the chutney |
| `andhra_idli_vada_sambar` | 9.1% | over | missed the vada |

**Every single one over-estimates, and the errors are largest on the
thinnest, most familiar items** — a single dosa, a single uthappam, a plate of
idli. On the North-Indian thalis and curries earlier in the corpus the error sat
at 7–20%. The pattern is: **the scanner is calibrated on rich, multi-component
dishes and badly off on a plain single item.**

The worst single failure is a **naming** one: `uthappam` is not recognised and
something is invented in its place. Invented dishes are the failure a user
cannot detect for themselves, so this matters more than the calorie number.

## What I am NOT doing about it

I am **not** writing a prompt rule about dosa and uthappam. The legume-rule
experiment (`notes/LEGUME_RULE_NEGATIVE_RESULT.md`) already showed that naming a
hard class of dish makes the model **hunt** for it and invent more. The same
failure mode is the most likely outcome of a "get South Indian food right"
paragraph, and it would be measured, not assumed.

Instead the next step is **coverage, not instructions**: batch 4 broadens the
corpus so the score stops being an artefact of which dishes I happened to find.
A prompt change is only worth attempting once the corpus is wide enough for an
improvement to mean something.

## CORPUS behind every number in this note

**17 cases over 15 photos, South-Indian breakfast heavy: 5 of the 17 are breakfast (masala dosa, rava idli, tamil uthappam, andhra idli vada sambar and the sambar plate), the rest North/North-West non-breakfast.** A breakfast-heavy corpus is a real bias and it is the reason this run's median (20.0%) is WORSE than batch 2's (15.4%) on an unchanged app — the two numbers are not comparable and the note says so.

**Distinct dish families in that corpus: 15 of 17 cases (dal x2, sambar x2).** So the case count OVERSTATES the evidence: 4 of 17 cases share a family, and a family's error is weighted once per case. Median error here is a median over cases, not over 15 independent judgements.
